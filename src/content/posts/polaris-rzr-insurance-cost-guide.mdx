# How Much Does Polaris RZR Insurance Cost? (2026 Real Numbers)

The most common question from RZR owners: "What will insurance cost me?" The answer depends on which RZR you own, how it's built out, how you use it, and which coverage type you choose.

Here's a practical, model-by-model breakdown with real 2026 cost ranges.

## RZR Model Overview: Value Drives Premium

Polaris RZR insurance premium is primarily driven by the agreed value of the machine — and RZR values range substantially across the lineup:

**RZR Trail (570/1000):** MSRP $14,000–$18,000. Entry-level RZR, primarily trail riding. Lower value means lower premium.

**RZR XP 1000:** MSRP $18,000–$25,000. The core performance RZR. Extremely popular with massive aftermarket support. With common modifications, build values commonly reach $25,000–$35,000+.

**RZR XP Turbo S / Turbo R:** MSRP $26,000–$36,000. Factory long-travel suspension, turbocharged engine, premium performance. Build values with mods commonly $35,000–$50,000+.

**RZR Pro R:** MSRP $40,000–$50,000. Polaris's premium RZR, 225HP, factory race-spec suspension. The most expensive production RZR. With aftermarket additions, $50,000–$70,000+ builds.

**RZR Pro XP:** MSRP $22,000–$30,000. The Sport model positioned between XP 1000 and Pro R. Strong modification platform.

## What Actually Drives Your RZR Insurance Premium

### 1. Agreed Value (The Biggest Driver)

Premium scales with the agreed value you establish for your machine. The difference between a $20,000 agreed value and a $45,000 agreed value on an XP 1000 is roughly $200–$300/year in premium.

This is why establishing accurate agreed value matters — not just for claim protection, but for premium appropriateness. Overvaluing the machine wastes premium. Undervaluing leaves you exposed.

For RZR builds, agreed value should include:
- Stock machine purchase price (or current market value for used machines)
- All aftermarket accessories at cost to replace new
- Professional installation labor if significant

### 2. Rider Age and Experience

Rider age is a significant underwriting factor for UTV policies. Insurers treat younger riders as statistically higher risk:

- Riders under 18: typically very expensive or not insurable on standard policies
- Riders 18–25: 30–50% surcharge over standard adult rates
- Riders 25–35: standard rates
- Riders 35+: standard to preferred rates

If you have multiple riders including younger family members, rider age affects premium. Named driver restrictions — identifying specific covered operators — can sometimes limit the impact.

### 3. How and Where You Ride

**Recreational trail riding:** Standard rate. This is the baseline for most UTV insurance.

**Desert and dune riding:** Some carriers view Glamis, sand dunes, and desert riding as higher risk due to rollover exposure on steep terrain. May carry a slight surcharge.

**Competition use:** The standard competition exclusion means this use isn't covered by standard policies. Carriers won't surcharge for declared competition use — they simply exclude it. If you want competition coverage, that's a separate product.

**Street-legal registration:** If your RZR is street-registered (possible in some states), the policy structure may change — you may need auto-style coverage for the on-road component and a UTV policy for off-road use.

### 4. Coverage Type: ACV vs. Agreed Value

The premium difference between an ACV policy and an agreed value policy on a $40,000 RZR is approximately $100–$200/year. That's the cost of the upgrade. The protection difference is enormous — at claim time, agreed value prevents the depreciation fight.

For new machines especially, the ACV vs. agreed value decision is stark. A brand new $35,000 RZR Turbo R depreciates rapidly in the first two years. An ACV claim in year two might return $24,000. Agreed value at $35,000 returns $35,000 (minus deductible). The $11,000 difference costs less than $200/year in additional premium.

### 5. Deductible Choice

Higher deductibles reduce premium:
- $500 deductible: standard rate
- $1,000 deductible: 5–10% premium reduction
- $2,500 deductible: 15–20% premium reduction

For RZR owners who can absorb a larger out-of-pocket on a single incident, raising the deductible to $1,000 or $2,500 makes economic sense over time.

### 6. Seasonal vs. Year-Round Coverage

RZR owners in northern states — Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan, Colorado above a certain elevation — often store their machines from November through March. Seasonal policies reduce the riding-season rate while maintaining comprehensive coverage (theft, storm) during storage.

A year-round rider in Arizona or California pays full premium 12 months. A seasonal rider in Wisconsin riding May–October might pay 50–60% of the year-round rate.

### 7. Storage and Security

Secure storage reduces theft risk and can reduce comprehensive premium:
- Locked garage: standard rate
- Locked, alarmed garage: modest discount with some carriers
- Open trailer in driveway: slight surcharge
- Regular travel with trailer to events: transport coverage needed

## How to Get the Best Rate on Your RZR

**1. Establish accurate agreed value.** Know what your build is actually worth. Overestimate and you pay too much premium. Underestimate and you're exposed at claim time.

**2. Build your documentation file.** Receipts, invoices, dated photos. This makes the agreed value conversation easier and the claim process faster.

**3. Get liability at $300K+.** The premium difference between $100K and $300K liability on a UTV is minimal — usually $20–$40/year. The protection difference for a serious injury claim is substantial.

**4. Compare specialty markets to standard carriers.** Standard carriers (Progressive, GEICO) offer decent baseline UTV policies. Specialty markets may offer better agreed value options, higher CPE limits, or more flexible coverage structures for built machines.

**5. Ask about multi-policy discounts.** Some carriers discount UTV policies when bundled with auto or homeowners. Worth asking.

**6. Consider your deductible.** If you're financially comfortable absorbing a $1,000–$2,500 out-of-pocket incident, a higher deductible reduces annual premium. Evaluate based on your actual financial position.
